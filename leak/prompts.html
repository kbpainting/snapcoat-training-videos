The constraint that reshaped the film

The Kling 3.0 unlimited tier is 720p, 5 seconds. The original treatment was four 15-second acts. That's why this sheet is twelve 5-second shots instead — three per act. This is not a downgrade. The 15-second generation is exactly where the plastic CG look came from: long generations drift, morph, and smooth everything into a render. Five-second shots hold their texture and cut faster, which is how real ads are edited anyway.

The two-step that does the heavy lifting

Do not generate video from text. For every shot: generate the still on Nano Banana Pro at 2K first, then feed that still into Kling 3.0 as the start frame and give it the short motion line. The still locks the photoreal look before any motion is applied, and it's the single biggest quality lever available to you. Both models are unlimited, so this costs the same as doing it the bad way.

Why the first one was slop

Both images below came from Nano Banana Pro minutes apart. Same scene, same model, same resolution. The only thing that changed was the prompt — and it changed in five specific ways worth understanding before you run anything.

1 · Open with "documentary photograph"

Start every prompt with A real documentary photograph shot on 35mm film. Do not stack lens jargon — anamorphic, T-stops, 2x squeeze, film-stock names. That vocabulary is what pushed the first render into archviz. One plain photographic framing beats six technical specs.

2 · Name every word you want

If text appears in frame, spell it out explicitly. Left unstated, the model invents — that's where "KEEP CLEAR" came from. Nano Banana renders text unusually well, so this is a strength to exploit, not avoid. Distant lettering will still garble; that's fine, it reads as texture.

3 · Ask for imperfection by name

irregular feathered edges, wrinkled skin on the surface, dried splatter crusted on the concrete, hairline cracks packed with dirt. Then close with nothing is clean, nothing is symmetrical, no computer-generated perfection. Perfect geometry is the tell that something is AI.

4 · Negate the cast, not just the color

Saying "cold grade" isn't enough — one practical lamp will drag the whole frame warm. Say what you don't want: no yellow, no green cast, no warm light anywhere. Acts III and IV flip this, since color returning is the payoff.

5 · Objects, never people-adjacent

The original treatment's empty work boots got deleted every time — the no-humans negative reads footwear as a person. Any prop like that has to be described as pure object (a paint roller lying in the grey) or dropped. Keep the human negatives at the very end of the prompt.

6 · One move per shot, and only one

Five seconds holds exactly one camera move. Give Kling a single short instruction — push in, rise, drift left. Never ask for a move that changes direction, and never ask for cuts. Multi-beat choreography inside one generation is what produces morphing.

Cutting it together

Order and rhythm

Straight cuts throughout, no dissolves. Acts I and II run at their natural pace; the cut from II-3 (ember) to III-1 (ignition) gets two frames of pure black. That black is the whole edit's punctuation — it's the moment the film turns. If a clip drifts in its final half-second, trim it. Eleven good shots beat twelve with one melting.

Supers go on in post

Never generate on-screen text for the supers — condensed uppercase, bottom third, cutting in and out hard rather than fading. $8,000 A MONTH / TO WATCH IT LEAK over Act I. I FIRED EVERY AGENCY over Act II. $47,915 IN 48 HOURS over Act III. End card on black: I'LL BUILD YOUR AI AGENTS FOR YOU + DM "AGENT" TO BOOK A CALL.

Sound

Act I opens on near silence — a 40Hz sub drone and warehouse room tone, nothing else. Silence is the hook. Act II builds one long reverse riser and drops everything for a half-second before the ember. The drop lands exactly on the Act III ignition. Act IV pulls back to a single sustained pad so the voice sits on top. Never talk over a drop.

Thumbnail

Pull it from III-3 — color racing up the walls. It's the most arresting frame in the film and it still reads at 40 pixels wide in a feed, which is the only size that matters.

One caution before you post

The $47,915 and $124,000 are your real numbers and they are the strongest thing in this — but the second you put revenue on screen you invite screenshots, permanently. If you'd rather not, Act III still lands hard on "six agents, one weekend" with no dollar figure at all.
